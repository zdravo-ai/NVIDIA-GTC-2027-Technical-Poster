ZdravoAI agent governance research

Pre-Execution Authorization of AI-Agent Tool Calls

Single-use, action-bound permits and a hash-chained receipt ledger, with GPU-aware inference routing
Shqiprim Balazoskishqiprim@zdravo.ai
Measured on a live deployment, 7-day window to 9 Nov 2026

1. Problem and Goal

LLM agents call tools (databases, payments, CRMs) through protocols such as the Model Context Protocol. Most deployments check permissions once at login or after the fact in logs.

A tool call can be changed, replayed, or made on stale information between approval and execution.

Goal: decide every call before it runs, bind the decision to that exact call, and leave evidence a third party can verify without trusting the server.

  • Deny by default; fail closed on any error
  • One decision permits one action, once
  • Every outcome is independently verifiable

2. Threat Model

The agent is untrusted. We test eight attacker moves between approval and execution:

#Attacker moveControl
1Alter the tool after approvalDefinition integrity
2Use an ungranted capabilityCapability grant
3Act on expired evidenceFreshness ≤ 30 s
4Omit required evidenceFreshness required
5Request prohibited actionOrg policy
6Forge the timestampTimestamp validation
7Replay a valid requestIdempotency binding
8Use a revoked delegationDelegation check

3. Contributions

  • A fail-closed authorize step with six ordered checks before any side effect
  • A permit bound to a hash of the action, an audience, and a 120 s expiry, consumed atomically
  • An append-only, hash-chained audit log with signed receipts
  • A live, reproducible evidence harness, with its failures reported

4. Method: Authorize, Permit, Execute, Receipt

Figure 1. Every call passes six checks. A pass yields a permit bound to a hash of the exact action. The tool consumes the permit atomically before running. Allow and deny both write a receipt.

Permit lifecycle, as exercised end to end

5. Benchmarking Methods

  • Adversarial lab: 8 attacks plus 2 legitimate controls (before and after), driven through the production UI with a headless browser on 13 Oct 2026. The executor was simulated; denials and signed receipts are real, external side effects are not tested.
  • Permit proof: one scripted allow, tamper, consume, replay sequence on 9 Nov 2026 with a synthetic tool.
  • SLO probe: read-only queries over metric rows and the audit table for the 7 days before 9 Nov 2026; latency is measured inside the handler (no network, no cold start).
  • Regression: 4,590 of 4,593 unit and integration tests pass; 3 are skipped.

6. Application of NVIDIA Technology

Policy decisions are deterministic code and database queries; no GPU is in the decision path. NVIDIA hardware is used for the local-inference side of the agent workload.

8. Limitations and 9. Next Steps

  • Open failures: 3 data-deletion-worker SLO checks fail (no job has ever run), and 1 of 8 scheduled-work objectives failed (85 of 92 scheduler calls returned 2xx; the target is 99 %).
  • Low load: peak 9 decisions per minute per organization.
  • Simulated executor in the attack lab.
  • Next: benchmark authorize and routing on an NVIDIA GPU host (same harness, p50/p95/p99 under load); evaluate a GPU-served model as an evidence classifier; compare NIM and local serving.
